Topic 1 of GS-3 (Indian Economy: Planning, Mobilization, Growth, Employment) 
1. Planning & Economic Strategy in India 
Quote: 
"A vision without a plan is just a wish." – Narendra Modi 
Introduction: 
India’s journey from centralised Five-Year Plans to Viksit Bharat@2047 reflects a shift towards mission-
mode governance and competitive federalism, guided by NITI Aayog’s reform-driven think tank model 
and “Sabka Prayas” philosophy. 
 
 
Examples: 
1. NITI Aayog released sector-specific strategy papers aligned with Viksit Bharat goals. 
2. State governments were ranked under SDG India Index, promoting outcome-based planning. 
3. Finance Commission incentivised states using performance-linked grants. 
Conclusion: 
Strategic planning must align vision with execution, ensuring economic reforms are federal, future-ready, 
and inclusive, building a roadmap for Amrit Kaal. 
2. Resource Mobilization: Taxation, Capital Markets, FDI, FPI 
Quote: 
"Revenue is the lifeblood of a government, transparency its heartbeat." — Inspired by the Kelkar Committee 
Introduction: 
With Make in India, Invest India, and Digital India as facilitators, India aims to boost resource mobilisation 
through efficient taxation, deepened capital markets, and stable foreign investments. 
Examples: 
1. Faceless tax regime and Vivad Se Vishwas scheme improved tax compliance.

3. Growth vs Development: Indicators, HDI, Inequality 
Quote: 
"Growth is hollow if it doesn’t bridge the divide." — Inspired by the Human Development Report

India’s development narrative balances GDP growth with improvement in HDI, GDI, and Multidimensional

Conclusion: 
India must prioritise inclusive indicators alongside GDP to ensure growth transforms into real, measurable 
and people-centric development.

In the world’s youngest nation, addressing structural unemployment and formalising the gig and informal

Examples:

"JAM is the highway to equity in a digital India." — Economic Survey

2. Budgeting, Fiscal Management & Investment Models 
1. Budget Process: Types, Components, Fiscal Deficit, FRBM Act 
Quote 
"A budget is not just numbers on a page, it is an embodiment of our values." – Barack Obama 
Intro 
India’s budget process reflects both constitutional fidelity (Article 112) and developmental foresight, 
structured around estimates, receipts, and expenditures. Guided by the FRBM Act, it aims to ensure 
macroeconomic stability while "planning prosperity through prudence." 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Examples 
• 
Interim budget focused heavily on revenue expenditure. 
• 
Fiscal deficit projected around 5.1% due to high subsidies. 
• 
16th Finance Commission asked to review FRBM targets. 
Conclusion 
A calibrated fiscal framework with an independent fiscal council can balance growth imperatives with 
discipline. 
2. Revenue & Capital Expenditure Trends 
Quote 
"Capital expenditure is the soul of sustainable growth." 
Intro 
India’s fiscal strategy is undergoing a shift from revenue-heavy budgets to capex-led growth engines, in line 
with the aim of “spending less and building more.” This approach enhances productivity, infrastructure, and 
private investment. 
Examples 
• 
Capex allocation increased to over ₹11 lakh crore for infra. 
• 
Fertilizer and food subsidies kept revenue outgo elevated. 
• 
PM Gati Shakti got enhanced capital infusion for logistics. 
Conclusion 
Capex focus must continue, coupled with efficient monitoring to ensure long-term returns and fiscal 
resilience.

2. Irrigation: Types, PMKSY, Watershed Development 
Quote 
"More crop per drop is not just a slogan, it's a necessity." 
Intro 
Efficient irrigation is the fulcrum of Indian agriculture. With only about 50% of gross cropped area irrigated, 
schemes like PMKSY and watershed development aim to bridge the "irrigation inclusion gap." 
Examples 
• 
Micro-irrigation coverage expanded under PMKSY-Per Drop More Crop. 
• 
Watershed Development Component aided drought-proofing in Bundelkhand. 
• 
Solar-powered pumps introduced under KUSUM for off-grid irrigation. 
Conclusion 
Scaling up micro and climate-resilient irrigation must be priority, with tech-driven water budgeting at the 
local level. 
3. Agri Marketing: eNAM, APMC reforms, GrAMs 
Quote 
"Farmer incomes rise not just from what they grow, but how and where they sell." 
Intro 
Agricultural marketing reforms are vital to move from price support to price realization. Initiatives like e-
NAM, Model APMC Acts, and GrAMs aim to create a “One Nation, One Market” ecosystem. 
Examples 
• 
Over 1,400 mandis integrated with e-NAM to enable inter-state trade. 
• 
States like Madhya Pradesh adopted APMC Act reforms for private markets. 
• 
GrAMs operationalized with digital kiosks and warehousing linkages. 
Conclusion 
India needs a unified agri-market architecture with robust logistics, digital access, and transparent pricing 
mechanisms.

“farm to fork efficiency.”

under PMFME for cold storage and

ONDC integration piloted for direct farm-
to-retail logistics. 
• 
NABARD supported warehouse projects 
through agri infra funds.

Post-harvest infrastructure must be localized, 
tech-integrated, and aligned with farmer 
producer organizations.

"MSP is not a price, it’s a promise of protection."

Quote 
"Turning grain into gain—food processing multiplies farm value." 
Intro 
Food processing is the missing link between farms and formal markets, with the potential to double farmer

Food processing sector contributes over 10% to manufacturing GVA.

Rise in demand for processed millets and organic produce boosted rural enterprise.

Unlocking the sector’s potential requires rural-centric industrial policy and skill-linked infrastructure.

Quote 
"The land question is the heart of rural justice." 
Intro 
Land reforms like zamindari abolition, tenancy regulation, and ceiling limits were designed to democratize 
land ownership and “redistribute dignity” in rural India. Yet, implementation gaps persist in several states.

Land ceiling redistribution remains below 2% of net sown area nationally.

Tenancy laws updated in Odisha and UP to legalize sharecropping. 
• 
Land rights regularization for forest dwellers implemented in tribal belts. 
Conclusion 
Future reforms must balance social equity with agricultural viability, focusing on legal clarity and 
inclusiveness.
