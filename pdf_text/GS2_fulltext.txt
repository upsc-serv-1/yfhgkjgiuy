GS-2 Topic 1: Indian Constitution & Polity 
1. Historical Underpinnings and Evolution of the Constitution 
Quote: 
"Our Constitution is a testament to the sacrifices of generations who believed in justice, liberty, equality, and 
fraternity." — President Droupadi Murmu 
Introduction: 
A journey from the Regulating Act to Nehru’s Objectives Resolution, India’s constitutional arc mirrored its 
transformation from colonial command to participatory democracy, embodying the spirit of “Raj se 
Samvidhan tak.” 
Examples: 
• 
Law Commission’s consultation paper proposed integrating constitutional history in legal education 
reforms 
• 
UGC rolled out ‘Samvidhan Mahotsav’ across universities 
• 
NCERT revamped Class 12 Political Science with new Constitution-making modules 
Conclusion: 
Constitutional literacy must deepen beyond ceremonies into classrooms, civic tech, and citizen charters to 
make Samvidhan truly participative. 
2. Salient Features and Basic Structure of the Constitution 
Quote: 
"The basic structure is the North Star of Indian democracy." — Justice D.Y. Chandrachud 
Introduction: 
Rooted in the ideals of liberty, equality, and fraternity, the Indian Constitution upholds “Nyay, not just 
Kanoon,” with its basic structure ensuring that democratic continuity outlasts political churn. 
Examples: 
• 
SC struck down electoral bonds citing violation of voters’ right to information 
• 
Parliamentary Committee reaffirmed secularism as core to electoral reforms 
• 
ARC-II emphasized constitutional morality as non-negotiable for institutional integrity 
Conclusion: 
India’s basic structure doctrine protects not tradition, but transformation with dignity, dissent, and democracy 
as its sentinels. 
3. Significant Provisions and Amendments 
Quote: 
"Amendment is the lifeline of a living Constitution." — Nani Palkhivala 
Introduction: 
India’s constitutional amendments from “One Nation, One Tax” 
to women-led Viksit Bharat demonstrate dynamic adaptation to 
justice, equity, and development goals.  
Examples: 
• 
106th Amendment enabled 33% reservation for women 
in legislatures 
• 
NITI Aayog proposed fiscal federalism reforms via 
constitutional amendment 
• 
SC upheld EWS quota as a reasonable classification under 
Art. 15(6) 
Conclusion: 
Amendments must be tools of inclusion, not instruments of

4. Comparison with Constitutions of Other Countries 
Quote: 
"While we borrowed from many, we built for Bharat." — Dr. B.R. Ambedkar  
Introduction: 
A unique blend of global constitutional wisdom, India’s constitutional design marries UK’s parliamentary 
ethics, US’s checks-and-balances, and Irish DPSPs to craft a Bharatiya model. 
Examples: 
• 
Law Commission proposed selective adoption of AI laws from EU 
• 
SC cited US Supreme Court precedents in digital privacy verdict 
• 
NITI Aayog referenced OECD models for cooperative federalism 
Conclusion: 
Learning globally but acting locally keeps Indian constitutionalism adaptive and culturally rooted. 
5. Separation of Powers and Dispute Redressal Mechanisms 
Quote: 
"No organ can claim supremacy, only harmony." — Justice R.F. Nariman 
Introduction: 
India’s democracy is safeguarded by a calibrated separation of powers, where checks and balances ensure that 
Samvaad, not Sangharsh, defines institutional relations. 
Examples: 
• 
SC ruling upheld tribunal independence in GST Appellate Tribunal setup 
• 
Lokpal released its first digital annual report 
• 
Ministry of Law proposed mediation bill reforms 
Conclusion: 
Checks and balances need constant tuning to preserve constitutional equilibrium. 
6. Federal Structure: Centre-State Relations and Challenges 
Quote: 
"Cooperative federalism is not a choice; it is a constitutional compulsion." — PM Narendra Modi 
Introduction: 
With platforms like NITI Aayog and PM Gati Shakti, India’s federal spirit is 
evolving from confrontation to convergence, ensuring “One India, Inclusive 
India.” 
Examples:  
• 
Finance Commission flagged GST compensation delays 
• 
Inter-State Council revived with new agenda 
• 
MoHUA enabled urban funds linked to SDG localisation 
Conclusion: 
India’s unity will depend on "Sahyog se Samriddhi", not just legal boundaries 
but fiscal trust and cooperative temperament.

Introduction: 
The post-war multilateral order faces a legitimacy deficit. As India aspires for Viksit Bharat@2047, its push for 
institutional reform from UNSC to WTO seeks to democratise global governance, ensuring that “rules-based 
order” is not code for status-quoism. 
Examples: 
• 
India backed Global Digital Public Infrastructure Framework at G20 
• 
UN Security Council reform pitch revived at South Summit chaired by India 
• 
WTO negotiations saw India defend Global South on agricultural subsidies 
Conclusion: 
Reimagining institutions is not disruption it’s inclusion, ensuring equity in global rule-making.
