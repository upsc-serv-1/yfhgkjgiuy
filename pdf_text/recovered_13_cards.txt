TOPIC-1. Indian Heritage and Culture 
A. Art Forms 
1. Classical Dances 
Quote: 
"Dance is the joy of the soul expressed through movement." — Rabindranath Tagore 
Introduction: 
Rooted in sacred texts, India’s classical dances have become a diplomatic tool under Ek Bharat Shreshtha Bharat and 
Indian Council for Cultural Relations, linking heritage with India’s growing global cultural stature. 
Examples: 
• 
Kuchipudi showcased at Shanghai cultural expo under Azadi Ka Amrit Mahotsav 
• 
Odissi included in India's pavilion at the World Tourism Expo 
Conclusion: 
Schemes like Kala Sanskriti Vikas Yojana and art integration in NEP 2020 are key to preserving traditional forms 
while fostering India’s cultural footprint globally. 
2. Folk Dances 
Quote: 
“The folk dance is the community’s way of telling its own story.” — Kapila Vatsyayan 
Introduction: 
Folk dances reflect agrarian rhythms, festivals, and social cohesion. Under Sanskritik Mahotsav and Virasat 
Programme, they’ve been revived as platforms for rural pride and community-led tourism. 
Examples: 
• 
Bihu featured in G20 cultural evening in Assam 
• 
Kalbelia integrated into District Cultural Mapping under Culture Mapping Portal 
Conclusion: 
Incentivizing folk forms via Scheme for Financial Assistance for Promotion of Art and Culture ensures cultural 
democracy and rural economic participation. 
3. Classical Music  
Quote: 
"Indian music is a meditation, not a performance." — Pandit Ravi Shankar 
Introduction: 
Indian classical music bridges temples, courts, and global stages. Supported by SPIC MACAY, IGNCA, and digital 
archiving efforts, it blends legacy with modern innovation and international engagement. 
Examples: 
• 
Ustad Amjad Ali Khan’s concerts included in India–Europe cultural diplomacy 
• 
Carnatic concerts integrated into cultural tourism circuits under Dekho Apna Desh 
Conclusion: 
Expanding Guru-Shishya Parampara Scheme and leveraging digital platforms ensures continuity of this rich sonic 
heritage in contemporary society. 
4. Folk Music 
Quote: 
"A nation’s folk music is its heartbeat." — Jawaharlal Nehru 
Introduction: 
Folk music preserves oral history and social memory. With renewed focus under NEP 2020 and Kala Utsav, it now 
enters classrooms and cultural policy to empower grassroots storytelling. 
Examples: 
• 
Manganiyars performed during National Teachers’ Conclave 
• 
Baul singers featured in India’s soft power delegation to ASEAN 
Conclusion: 
Archiving traditions and artist cooperatives, backed by Sangeet Natak Akademi and IGNCA, will prevent erosion of 
these living narratives.

Quote: 
“Good governance depends on ability, not birth.” – Jawaharlal Nehru 
Intro: 
The British laid the foundations of centralized administration in India through regulatory and legislative acts, 
shaping civil services and institutions that influenced India's future governance structure and federal setup. 
Examples: 
• 
Indian Civil Services legacy continues through UPSC's role in selecting public administrators. 
• 
Government of India Act, 1935 served as the template for India's constitutional architecture. 
Conclusion: 
Though colonial in intent, these reforms enabled India’s administrative continuity post-1947. Mission 
Karmayogi builds on this legacy for a citizen-centric bureaucracy. 
2. Land Revenue Systems: Zamindari, Ryotwari, Mahalwari 
Quote: 
“The misery hardly finds vent in words… it is so deep.” – Rabindranath Tagore 
Intro: 
British land revenue systems like Zamindari, Ryotwari, and Mahalwari prioritized revenue over rural welfare, 
dismantling community land rights and creating structural rural poverty that still echoes in land debates today. 
Examples: 
• 
Digitisation of land records under Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme tackles legacy 
issues from colonial land mapping. 
• 
PM Kisan Samman Nidhi offers income support to small farmers in a system once devoid of state 
welfare. 
Conclusion: 
Colonial systems extracted without empowering. Land and income-support schemes aim to reverse historical 
marginalisation and ensure agrarian dignity. 
3. Economic Impact of British Rule 
Quote: 
“India is a mere agricultural farm for England a nursery of soldiers.” – Dadabhai Naoroji 
Intro: 
Colonial policies reoriented India from a producer of crafts to a supplier of raw materials, resulting in 
deindustrialisation, trade imbalance, and impoverishment  the foundation of the 'Drain of Wealth' theory. 
Examples: 
• 
One District One Product initiative revives crafts once destroyed by colonial trade policies. 
• 
PLI Scheme (Production Linked Incentive) boosts manufacturing to reclaim India's industrial self-
reliance. 
Conclusion: 
The colonial economy weakened local enterprise. Current industrial policies aim to rebuild India’s lost 
productive capacity. 
4. Social and Cultural Impact 
Quote: 
“No people can be truly free unless they are educated.” – Swami Vivekananda 
Intro: 
British rule introduced Western education, new laws, and cultural changes sparking both social reform and 
resistance. It seeded modern institutions but also fragmented traditional knowledge systems. 
Examples: 
• 
National Education Policy promotes mother tongue instruction a shift from colonial language 
imposition.
