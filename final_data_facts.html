PRE-COLONIAL PERIOD & CHRONOLOGY

DATA: Historical (1498-1792) & 2024 (Antiquities)
Culture: Artefacts smuggling
The US returned 1,400 looted artifacts, valued at nearly $10 million, to India (Nov 2024)
India reclaimed 297 antiquities during PM Modi's recent US visit, raising the total recoveries since 2014 to 640 (Sep 2024).
Economy during Mughal period
GDP in the 16th century (1600 AD) -> ~22.5–25.1% of the world economy - 2nd largest in the world (Angus Maddison, The World Economy: Historical Statistics).
Annual revenue of Akbar's treasury in 1600 AD at £17.5 million (in contrast to the entire treasury of Great Britain two hundred years later in 1800 AD, which totalled £16 million).
India experienced unprecedented prosperity in history.
Advent of Europeans
the Portuguese, the first Europeans to come to India (1498), were also the last to leave this land (1961).
Third Anglo-Mysore War: Tipu Sultan had to pay Rs.3 Crore as war indemnity. He also had to surrender 2 of his sons as surety to the British till he paid his due.

BRITISH COLONIAL ECONOMY

DATA: Historical (1664-1950) & 2025 (Oxfam Report)
Economic drain by British
Scholars:
Romesh Chandra Dutt: In early 20th century ≈ £20 million flew out of India.
MG Ranade: In his book "Essay on Indian Economics," he claimed that Britishers drained more than one-third of India's wealth into England.
Dadabhai Naoroji: India's tax burden in 1886 was 14.3% of total revenue, significantly higher than England's 6.93%.
Angus Maddison (British economist): India's share of the world income went from 27% in 1700 AD (compared to Europe's share of 23%) to 3% in 1950.
Reports:
'Takers Not Makers: The Unjust Poverty and Unearned Wealth of Colonial Inheritance' report, 2025 (Oxfam):
Wealth Drain during Colonial Period -> Between 1765 and 1900, UK drained $64.82 trillion from India, with $33.8 trillion going to the top 10%.
India and its Contradictions: In the 18th century, India became a land of contrasts because extreme poverty and extreme luxury existed side by side.
Famines
Between 1730 to 1947 → 31 famines, loss of life of 8.5 cr. Indians
In 1770, there was a terrible Famine in Bengal which killed about 1 Crore people.

Land Revenue systems
Permanent Settlement:
Covered 20% of British India
The state received 10/11th of the rent the zamindars received from the peasantry, with only 1/11th going to the zamindars themselves.
Between 1794 and 1819, 68% of zamindari land was sold in Bengal.
Ryotwari System:
Covered 51% area of British India.
Revenue was fixed at very high rate (45-50%)
State of Industry
Up until 1750, India produced about 25% of the world's industrial output.
India had a 25% share of the global textile trade in the early 18th century.
By 1664, the English East India Company imported more than 750,000 pieces of cotton goods from India, which accounted for 73 per cent of the Company’s total trade.
Infrastructure
On April 16, 1853 -> first railway line in India spanning upto 34 kilometers between Boribandar (Bombay) to Thane.
Built 6000 kms of track by 1869 and 45,000 km of track by 1905 with the help of private enterprises.
Railway length = ~69,439 route km (~1.32 lakh track km) today (Economic Survey 2025-26) --> ~53,000–54,000 km built during British rule before 1947 (Ministry of Railways).

COLONIAL POLICIES & DEVELOPMENT

DATA: Historical (1773-1947)
Administrative Reforms
Cornawallis Reforms:
all higher posts in administration worth more than 500 pounds a year in salary could be held by Englishmen only.
Charter Act of 1793 had reserved all posts worth 500 pounds per annum for the covenant servants of the Company.
Salary of the Collector of a district was raised to Rs. 1500 a month and 1 per cent commission on the revenue collected from his district.
Viscount Lee Commission recommendations (1924): Parity between Indians and Europeans: To be achieved 50:50 parity in 15 years.
Morley-Minto Reforms: number of members in the Central Legislative Council was raised from 16 to 60.
Provinces: 3 provinces in 1773 to 17 provinces in 1947.
Social Development
Status of illiteracy: ~94% in 1911 (~6% literacy) and ~92% in 1921 (~8% literacy; ~84% illiteracy at Independence in 1947) (Census of British India / NCERT)
Number of Universities:
1857: 3 (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras)
1947: 19 universities, ~500 colleges
Age of Consent Act(1891): Raised age of consent for girls from 10 to 12 years.
Child Marriage Restraint Act (1929): Minimum marriage age: 14 (girls) and 18 (boys).
Gandhi ji campaign for untouchability: Gandhi’s campaign against untouchability which covered 12,500 miles and lasted for nine months.
Poona Pact: Number of seats reserved for the depressed classes in provincial legislatures would be increased from 71 in the Award to 147; and to 18% of the total in central legislature.
Rabindranath Tagore wrote around 2230 songs.

Various stages of Freedom struggle

FREEDOM STRUGGLE & MOVEMENTS

DATA: Historical (1857-1937)
Revolt of 1857
European Soldier to Indian soldier ratio: 1:2
Revolt of 1857 -> Proportion of Europeans to Indians was carefully fixed at 1:2 in the Bengal Army and 2:5 in the Madras and Bombay Armies to avoid another revolt (Peel Commission, 1859 / NCERT).
In Awadh, more than 20,000 taluqdars had their estates confiscated
Bengal Partition (1905)
Reason given for Partition: Population of 78 million Bengal had become too big to be administered.
Jallianwala Bagh
House of Lords endorsed Reginald Dyer’s action and the British public showed solidarity with him by helping collect 30,000 pounds for him for his atrocities in Jallianwala Bagh.
Non-Cooperation Movement (1920 to 1922)
1st nationwide movement launched by Gandhiji in 1920 during British rule.
During Non-cooperation Movement:
Thousands of students left schools and colleges and joined only 800 national schools and colleges.
Rs 1 crore were raised for Tilak Swaraj Fund by August 1921, and 1 crore/50 lakhs members got enrolled in Congress.
Consequence of Non-Cooperation Movement: The import of foreign goods halved between 1921 and 1922.
Home Rule League Movement
Tilak’s Home Rule League had 6 Branches while Annie Besant’s Home rule League had 200+ Branches.
Civil Disobedience Movement (1930)
Gandhi along with a band of 78 members of Sabarmati Ashram, marched from his headquarters in Ahmedabad for 240 miles.
On April 23, 1930, the arrest of Congress leaders in the NWFP led to the participation of a 92% Muslim population.
Gandhi’s Eleven demands -> Reduce Rupee-Sterling Exchange Ratio to 1s 4d: Called for a change in the exchange rate between the Indian rupee and the British pound to benefit Indian traders and businesses.
1937 Elections
1937 elections were held in 11 provinces- Madras, United Provinces, Central Provinces, Bihar, Orissa, Assam, NWFP, Bengal, Punjab, Bombay and Sindh
In 1937 elections, An estimated 30.1 million persons, including 4.25 million women, had acquired the right to vote (12% of the total population), and 15.5 millions of these, including 917,000 women, participated to exercise their franchise.
In 1937 elections, Among the 864 seats assigned “general” constituencies, Congress contested 739 and won 617.
