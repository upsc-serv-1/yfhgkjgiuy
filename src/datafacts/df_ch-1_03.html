BRITISH COLONIAL ECONOMYCONTINUED
Famines
Between 1730 to 1947 → 31 famines, loss of life of 8.5 cr. Indians
In 1770, there was a terrible Famine in Bengal which killed about 1 Crore people.
Land Revenue systems
Permanent Settlement:
Covered 20% of British India
The state received 10/11th of the rent the zamindars received from the peasantry, with only 1/11th going to the zamindars themselves.
Between 1794 and 1819, 68% of zamindari land was sold in Bengal.
Ryotwari System:
Covered 51% area of British India.
Revenue was fixed at very high rate (45-50%)
State of Industry
Up until 1750, India produced about 25% of the world's industrial output.
India had a 25% share of the global textile trade in the early 18th century.
By 1664, the English East India Company imported more than 750,000 pieces of cotton goods from India, which accounted for 73 per cent of the Company’s total trade.
Infrastructure
On April 16, 1853 -> first railway line in India spanning upto 34 kilometers between Boribandar (Bombay) to Thane.
Built 6000 kms of track by 1869 and 45,000 km of track by 1905 with the help of private enterprises.
Railway length = ~69,439 route km (~1.32 lakh track km) today (Economic Survey 2025-26) --> ~53,000–54,000 km built during British rule before 1947 (Ministry of Railways).

COLONIAL POLICIES & DEVELOPMENT

DATA: Historical (1773-1947)
Administrative Reforms
Cornawallis Reforms:
all higher posts in administration worth more than 500 pounds a year in salary could be held by Englishmen only.
Charter Act of 1793 had reserved all posts worth 500 pounds per annum for the covenant servants of the Company.
Salary of the Collector of a district was raised to Rs. 1500 a month and 1 per cent commission on the revenue collected from his district.
Viscount Lee Commission recommendations (1924): Parity between Indians and Europeans: To be achieved 50:50 parity in 15 years.
Morley-Minto Reforms: number of members in the Central Legislative Council was raised from 16 to 60.
Provinces: 3 provinces in 1773 to 17 provinces in 1947.
Social Development
Status of illiteracy: ~94% in 1911 (~6% literacy) and ~92% in 1921 (~8% literacy; ~84% illiteracy at Independence in 1947) (Census of British India / NCERT)
Number of Universities:
1857: 3 (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras)
1947: 19 universities, ~500 colleges
