Mercury pollution
India- 2nd largest mercury emitter -> 144.7 tonnes Hg/year
India imports over 250 metric tonnes of mercury each year
India leaks > 220 tonnes into the environment from known sources (Toxics Link, a NGO)
> 70% to 80% of India's mercury emissions due to coal usage.
Global position
Current levels in the atmosphere are up to 500 % above natural levels.
In Oceans: 200 % above natural levels.
<b>4. Land pollution: 33% of the country’s total geographic area —are affected by soil</b> degradation, salinity, and vegetation loss.
Noise pollution
<b>Optimum noise level (WHO)</b>
45 dB during the day and 35 dB at night.
Anything above 80 dB is hazardous.
12,000 premature deaths and 48,000 new cases of ischaemic heart disease every year (European Environment agency)
Noise level of >80 decibels for more than 8 hours a day increases changes in breathing patterns (WHO)
Light pollution
Artificial light increasing by around 2% per year globally.
Biomedical waste
India generates 743 tonnes of biomedical waste daily and about 640 tonnes is treated (CPCB report)
Waste to Energy conversion
Total waste: 65 million tonnes of waste every year;
estimated to go up to 165 million tonnes by 2030 and
436 million tonnes by 2050
<b>Conversion Potential</b>
1700 MW from urban waste (1500 from MSW and 225 MW from sewage) and
about 1300 MW from industrial waste [Ministry of New and Renewable Energy]
Current status: 75% of municipal waste gets collected; only 22 to 28 percent is processed
12 operational and eight non-operational waste-to-energy plants in India in 10 states as of November 2022.
Solid waste management
India generated 170,000 tonnes of municipal solid waste per day
Collection and treatment: About 156,000 tonnes were collected, of which nearly 54 per cent was treated, and
Deposition in landfills: 24% was deposited in landfills (CPCB 2024).
<b>Type of waste</b>
50-55% of waste in Indian cities is biodegradable wet waste;
35% is non-biodegradable wet waste; 10% is inert.
Per capita waste generation -> B/w 0.2 Kg to 0.6 Kg per day in cities with population ranging from 1.0 lakh to 50 lakhs.
Projections: Indian cities will generate approximately 435 million tonnes of solid waste by 2050 (MoHUA 2021).
